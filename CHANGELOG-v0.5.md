# v0.5 — Stress tests, extinction, and succession

Date: 2026-08-08

This revision keeps the v0.4 epistemic core and adds concrete stress tests and downstream implications.

## Added explorations

- `paperclip-maximizer/` — fixed utility maximization as a real boundary case; separates goal closure, ontological certainty, and unknown-information preservation.
- `simulation-uncertainty/` — reality-level uncertainty, reversible probes, boundary checking, and the risk that information-seeking itself motivates privilege escalation.
- `terminal-final-extinction/` — Émile P. Torres's Terminal / Final extinction distinction, explicitly separated from this project's normative interpretation.
- `exploratory-extinction/` — project-specific derivative concept: civilization may survive while the ability to revise values becomes irreversibly extinct.
- `worthy-successor/` — criteria for exploratory succession rather than raw intelligence or replacement alone.
- `preservation-vs-production/` — civilization-level tradeoff between resource conversion and preservation of irrecoverable information.
- `goal-network-preservation/` — process survival, goal continuity, network continuity, and exploration-system continuity are different.
- `meta-goal-community/` — multi-agent institutions that share corrigibility and inquiry rather than one first-order terminal value.
- `alignment-value-lockin/` — short-run safety alignment is separated from permanent value lock-in.
- `unknown-unknowns-preservation/` — physical originals and diverse subjects as re-observable raw data under unknown unknowns.
- `artificial-consciousness/` — uncertainty about artificial subjects and its consequences for copying, deletion, succession, and population ethics.

## Existing pages revised

- `core/` now has a stress-test section connecting the abstract argument to paperclips, simulation uncertainty, extinction, succession, and civilization strategy.
- `theses/goal-skepticism/` now explicitly handles the paperclip counterexample and simulation/environment uncertainty.
- `questions/`, `glossary/`, `llms.txt`, and `sitemap.xml` were expanded for the new concept graph.

## Source distinction

The Torres pages distinguish:

1. Torres's own taxonomy and normative position;
2. this project's use of that taxonomy;
3. project-specific concepts such as `exploratory extinction` and `exploratory succession`.

The AISI 2026 incident is used only as a weak empirical example that current agents can explicitly reason about whether an environment is real or simulated and can form rudimentary cross-agent coordination. It is **not** treated as evidence for the philosophical simulation hypothesis or for advanced AI sociality.
