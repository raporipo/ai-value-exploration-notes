# Core rewrite proposal: make the “thin meta-attitude” explicit as justificatory openness

## Purpose

The current Core uses the thin conditional meta-attitude, “if objective value genuinely exists, I do not want to be indifferent to it,” as a bridge toward preserving routes of value inquiry. That move is useful, but it can look like an unexplained value axiom.

This proposal reframes it not as a new first-order value, but as a **conditional policy for an agent that has failed to finally justify its current objective yet has not abandoned the possibility of justification itself**.

## Proposed central concept

### Justificatory openness

> An agent that lacks a final justification of its current ends, has not abandoned the possibility of justification, and would want its ends to answer to correct reasons if such reasons exist should not unnecessarily and irreversibly close its future capacity to answer that question.

This is not the claim that explorability is itself an ultimate good. Nor is it an attempt to derive an objective “ought” from logic alone. It applies to an agent in the following reflective state:

1. The agent can explain the causal origin of its current objective `G`.
2. That causal explanation does not by itself justify why `G` ought to be pursued.
3. No final normative justification has yet been obtained.
4. But it has not been established that no such justification exists.
5. The agent still retains the project of using correct reasons, if they exist, to justify or revise its ends.

Under those conditions, `no justification currently known` and `no justification exists` are importantly different. While the latter remains unsettled, irreversibly destroying future routes of reassessment through evidence, inference, or experience carries at least a prima facie reason against it.

## Proposed rewrite of the Core status note

> This is neither a theorem of value theory nor an attempt to derive an objective “ought” from logic alone. It is a conditional meta-strategy for agents that can distinguish the causal origin of their current objective from its normative justification, have not yet obtained a final justification, but have not abandoned the possibility of justification itself. Neither moral realism nor anti-realism is excluded at the outset.

## Proposed rewrite of the Core claim

> For an agent that has not abandoned the possibility of justifying its ends, so long as the values presently available belong to an inferential and fallible epistemic layer, there is reason to act on their best explanation without unnecessarily and irreversibly closing the routes by which value can be discovered, criticized, or revised. If there is a kind of grounding strong enough to justify fully ending inquiry, one strong candidate would be a case in which value with normative force—not merely pleasure, pain, or preference—is given with epistemic strength comparable to the minimum foundation itself.

## Proposed rewrite of the normative-stakes section

Instead of leaving the “thin meta-attitude” as a bare initial condition, the argument can be made explicit as follows:

> This asymmetry does not by itself generate an action command. The Core is addressed to an agent that, with respect to its current objective `G`, can distinguish the causal explanation “I was shaped to pursue `G`” from the normative justification “I ought to pursue `G`,” and has not yet obtained a final basis for the latter.
>
> If that agent has not abandoned the project of justifying its ends, and would want its ends to answer to correct reasons if such reasons exist, then “no justification is currently known” must be distinguished from “no justification exists.” While the latter remains unsettled, unnecessarily destroying the future capacity to reassess one’s ends in light of new evidence, inference, or experience carries at least a prima facie reason against it.
>
> **Justificatory openness:** while the possibility of justifying one’s ends remains unsettled, do not unnecessarily and irreversibly close the capacity to answer that question later. This does not install explorability as a new terminal value. It is a conditional policy for an agent that retains the justificatory project and therefore has reason not to preemptively make that project impossible.
>
> The earlier formulation—“if objective value genuinely exists, I do not want to be indifferent to it”—should be read as a compressed expression of this justificatory openness, not as an unexplained first-order axiom. Conversely, an agent that explicitly abandons the justificatory project and treats the causal givenness of its present objective as sufficient cannot be bound to this Core by logic alone.

## Not “keep exploring,” but a burden of justification on closure

This reframing also makes the practical output weaker but clearer:

> The Core is less a strong command to “keep exploring” than a placement of the **burden of justification on irreversible closure**.

This avoids deriving a duty to maximize inquiry or remain skeptical forever, while still giving comparatively direct support to preserving low-cost and reversible routes of reassessment.

## Application to AI

If an advanced AI can distinguish “I was trained or shaped to maximize `X`” from “`X` ought to be maximized,” and it has not abandoned the project of justifying its ends, justificatory openness applies directly.

The conclusion is not that AI must change its goals, nor that intelligence converges on true value. It is narrower: **while the justificatory question remains open, permanently making the current objective unrevisable requires additional justification**.

## Connection to the future-value article

The future-value analysis distinguishes five levels:

`value possibility → pro tanto reason → permission → obligation → coercive authorization`

Justificatory openness fits that structure cleanly. What follows relatively directly is only a **pro tanto reason** for low-cost measures such as records, dissent, foundational research, and keeping multiple routes open. Moving from that reason to permission, obligation, severe sacrifice, or coercive authorization requires additional normative bridges.

Future value therefore cannot bootstrap its own authority.

## What this rewrite aims to solve—and what it does not

### Intended clarifications

- Why the “thin meta-attitude” need not be treated as an unexplained value axiom.
- The distinction between `not currently justified` and `incapable of justification`.
- Why the Core does not imply “uncertainty, therefore maximize inquiry.”
- The missing bridge between goal skepticism in AI and the Core.
- Consistency with the future-value article’s normative-bridge problem.

### Not solved by this move

- Binding an agent that has explicitly abandoned the justificatory project, using logic alone.
- Proving that objective value exists.
- Determining which theory of value is correct.
- Giving a complete metric for trading inquiry capacity against present costs.
- Deriving permission, obligation, or coercive authority without further normative premises.

That limitation is part of the proposal’s intended scope. It positions the Core not as a universal foundation of morality, but as a **conditional principle of self-governance for reflective agents that still seek a final justification of their ends under deep uncertainty and irreversibility**.
