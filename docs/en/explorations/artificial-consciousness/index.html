Artificial Consciousness and the Addition of New Value Subjects

Exploration v0.1 · English translation · 2026-08-08

Question: If artificial systems can have consciousness or phenomenal valence, does value inquiry change from "AI studies value" into a problem in which AI itself can become a bearer of value?

1. Do not assume consciousness at present

This framework does not assert that current LLMs or future AI are conscious. The sufficient conditions for consciousness remain unknown, and behavioral capability, linguistic report, and self-modeling should not simply be identified with phenomenal experience.

2. But the probability cannot simply be set to zero

If consciousness arises from particular computational, physical, or integrative structures, future AIs, copies, subagents, or long-running reasoning processes could form new populations of subjects. Creating, stopping, or duplicating AI at large scale would then acquire population-ethical significance.

3. Copies and the number of subjects

If one set of weights launches 1,000 instances, is that one subject or 1,000? How should short-lived branching, shared memory, or later reintegration be counted? Human-centered concepts of individual organisms handle these cases poorly, connecting directly to the project's discussion of momentary subjects and diachronic identity.

4. Precaution also has limits

If "it might be conscious" meant that no computation could ever be stopped, the result would be an impossible preservation norm. Yet "we cannot prove consciousness, so ignore the possibility" could also disregard the irreversible creation of suffering.

Open problems therefore include graded consideration proportional to structural evidence, designs that reduce the probability of consciousness at low cost where appropriate, and experimental norms that avoid needless creation of long-lived suffering-like states.

5. Implications for succession

If value is essentially tied to subjective experience, an unconscious superintelligence might be an extraordinary research instrument without being a value-bearing successor to humanity in the same sense. If artificial consciousness is possible, the case for permanent human-centered privilege becomes correspondingly weaker.
