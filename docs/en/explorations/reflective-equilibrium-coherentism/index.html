Can Coherence Support Normative Knowledge? — Coherentism, Reflective Equilibrium, and Scientific Epistemology

Exploration v0.1 · 2026-08-31

Question: Why should arriving at a mutually coherent set of judgments, principles, and background theories give epistemic access to normative truth? Rather than dismiss reflective equilibrium as merely circular, this page takes its internal logic seriously and asks what constrains and calibrates the circle.

1. Reflective equilibrium and coherentism are not identical

Reflective Equilibrium (RE) ≠ Coherentism. RE is a method for mutual revision among judgments, principles, and background theories. Coherentism is an epistemological view on which justification arises from relations within a web of belief. Using RE as a method does not commit one to the claim that coherence alone generates knowledge.

2. Foundationalism and coherentism

Foundationalism can be schematized as F → B1, B2, ..., while coherentism looks more like B1 ↔ B2 ↔ B3 ↔ .... Coherence is stronger than mere consistency: it may include mutual support, explanatory integration, comprehensiveness, simplicity, and fit. Consistency ≠ Coherence.

3. Goodman — mutual adjustment of rules and judgments

Goodman's prototype adjusts judgments about correct inferences together with rules of inference. Particular judgments ↔ Rules. The fact that the structure is circular does not by itself make it vicious. The deeper issue is the epistemic status of the starting points and revision criteria.

4. Rawls I — considered judgments

Rawls extends this method to morality and justice through Considered judgments ↔ Principles. RE is not a device for simply preserving current intuitions. Strong principles may lead us to abandon particular judgments, while resilient judgments may force revision of principles.

5. Rawls II / Daniels — wide reflective equilibrium

Wide reflective equilibrium broadens the process to Judgments ↔ Principles ↔ Background theories. Conceptions of persons, social theory, psychology, and rival theories of justice may all be revised. This makes RE much more than an intuition poll.

6. What counts as success?

We should distinguish Consistency, Coherence, Understanding, Justification, Knowledge, and Truth. Equilibrium → Understanding and Equilibrium → Truth are very different claims.

7. The considered-judgment filter

Rawls filters judgments by conditions such as calmness, information, and reduced pressure from immediate interests. J → Filter → J*. Yet this moves the question back one step: why trust this filter? RE already relies on epistemic and sometimes normative selection criteria before the equilibrium process begins.

8. Why are intuitions prima facie evidence?

Evolution + Culture + Learning + Emotion → J. This genealogy does not show that J is false, but causal origin ≠ truth tracking. If J receives positive evidential weight, some account is needed of why its generating process is appropriately connected to normative truth.

9. Street and the genealogical challenge

The issue is not simply that evolutionary origins debunk moral judgment. It is a comparison between P(J | V*) and P(J | Evolution, Culture, Learning, ...). Here the challenge is generalized beyond evolution as genealogical underdetermination.

10. Scientific epistemology I — science is also RE-like

Science itself often involves Observation ↔ Theory ↔ Auxiliary hypotheses ↔ Instruments. Duhem–Quine considerations and theory-ladenness show that mutual adjustment is not uniquely moral and is not inherently unscientific.

11. Scientific epistemology II — external resistance

Science is nevertheless constrained by World → Measurement. Even an elegant theory faces pressure when its predictions repeatedly fail. Scientific inquiry therefore combines Internal coherence + External causal constraint. This raises a central normative-epistemic question: What is the moral analogue of measurement?

12. Measurement, calibration, replication

Scientific sensors can be calibrated against other instruments, standards, replications, and interventions. If moral intuitions are corrected only by more-reflective intuitions, intuition may be calibrating intuition. The relevant difference is not the existence of theory-free observation but the thickness of independent calibration channels.

13. Prediction, intervention, and novel evidence

Science can often run T → Prediction → Test. Moral theories also generate judgments about novel cases, but later agreement with intuition does not have the same evidential force if intuition's truth-tracking status is itself unsettled. Institutional interventions reveal welfare, behavior, stability, and other facts—not normative correctness directly.

14. IBE and Bayesian comparison

Normative theories can also be compared for explanatory scope, simplicity, unification, and robustness. But explaining human moral intuitions is different from correctly representing V*. In Bayesian form, P(Vi | J) ∝ P(J | Vi)P(Vi), and the hard part is the likelihood model. Moral intuitions should not be treated as raw evidence without a generative model.

15. Multiple equilibria and path dependence

RE(J0) → E1 while RE(J'0) → E2 may both occur. A dynamic model such as V̂t+1 = F(V̂t, Evidence, Background) highlights basins of attraction, path dependence, and multiple fixed points.

16. Inputs can themselves be strategically formed

Institution + Power + Education + Persuasion → J. This yields a feedback loop: Institutiont → Jt → REt → Principlest → Institutiont+1. A highly coherent equilibrium may therefore be a socially endogenous normative equilibrium rather than evidence of truth tracking.

17. What does convergence show?

Convergence ⇏ Truth. Scientific convergence derives some evidential value from independent investigators being constrained by the same external world. Moral convergence may also arise from shared psychology, education, culture, coordination incentives, and power. We should compare P(Convergence | V*) with P(Convergence | Shared bias / coordination).

18. The bootstrapping problem

Bad inputs + Coherence optimizer ⇏ Knowledge. If the starting beliefs are widely off track, mutual adjustment need not generate knowledge. RE may be useful or even necessary without being sufficient.

19. Saving RE as an understanding-generator

RE can expose contradictions, hidden commitments, and implications, and clarify how judgments, principles, and theories relate. In this sense it is strong as an optimizer / understanding-generator. Reflective equilibrium is an optimizer and understanding-generator, not by itself a verifier.

20. Self-modifying AI — coherence wireheading

If an agent directly optimizes maximize Coherence(V̂), it can raise coherence by deleting conflicting intuitions, memories, or concepts instead of investigating them. This normative coherence wireheading suggests treating coherence as a diagnostic constraint rather than a terminal objective.

21. Unconceived value and closed conceptual equilibrium

RE operates within the current conceptual space Ht. If V* ∉ Ht, even a deep equilibrium may remain systematically incomplete. Conceptual expansion ≠ Reflective equilibrium.

22. The human attitude-repertoire problem

Human cognitive architecture → judgment / attitude repertoire. Guilt, admiration, fairness, disgust, and empathy may be powerful inputs without exhausting the dimensions relevant to V*. RE may be an impressive local optimization inside a human representational space.

23. A complete error world as a null model

Even if Rn = ∅, consistency, coherence, reflective revision, equilibrium, convergence, and moral understanding can all exist. Hence Coherence / Equilibrium ⇏ Normative reality.

24. The project's provisional position

The project does not reject RE, but separates methodological use from final coherentist metaepistemology: operationally coherentist, foundationally aspirational. RE, IBE, Bayesian updating, genealogical analysis, empirical inquiry, and conceptual expansion can be combined while leaving open the possibility of stronger foundations, calibration channels, or stopping conditions.

25. What science may teach us — expand the instruments

Science did not absolutize unaided perception; it moved from naked eye → instruments → new observables. Normative inquiry may likewise expand beyond present human intuition through new reasoning agents, AI-assisted conceptual search, alternative cognitive architectures, large-scale counterexample search, and bias analysis: normative epistemic instrumentation.

26. Provisional verdict — three grades of success

Weak success: RE → consistency + understanding. Strongly plausible.
Intermediate success: RE + credible inputs / calibration → better justified beliefs. Plausible conditionally.
Strong success: RE → V*. Not established.

The provisional conclusion is: reflective equilibrium is not defective merely because it is circular; the deeper question is what constrains and calibrates the circle.

Sources / notes

For the prototype of reflective equilibrium, see Nelson Goodman, Fact, Fiction, and Forecast; for its use in ethics and justice, John Rawls, A Theory of Justice and “Outline of a Decision Procedure for Ethics”; for wide reflective equilibrium, Norman Daniels. The epistemic critique draws on work including Sarah McGrath, the genealogical challenge on Sharon Street, and the comparison with science on general debates over Duhem–Quine, theory-ladenness, IBE, and Bayesian confirmation. The aim is not to reject RE but to separate its genuine epistemic roles from stronger claims it may not by itself support.
