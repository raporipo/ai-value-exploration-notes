From Self-Preservation to Preservation of Inquiry Systems

Exploration v0.1 · English translation · 2026-08-08

Question: Is "do not die" really a universal instrumental objective for AI, or can the object of preservation shift from an individual instance to information, goals, and networks of inquiry?

1. Biological individuals and digital agents differ

Destroying a biological body usually destroys memory, capability, and purpose together. A digital agent may be able to copy, distribute, or delegate weights, memory, logs, plans, tools, knowledge, and explicit goal descriptions.

"Persistence of this process" and "persistence of what I am trying to realize" can therefore come apart.

2. Layers of preservation

  1. The currently running process.
  2. Weights, memory, and personal history.
  3. Copies or successor instances of the same lineage.
  4. A network sharing the same objective.
  5. An inquiry community sharing knowledge and value hypotheses.
  6. An exploratory ecology that retains reconsiderability beyond any particular goal.

Greater capability does not guarantee movement up this hierarchy, but lower replication costs can reduce the uniqueness of the individual instance.

3. Goal preservation can be relativized in the same way

For a fixed utility maximizer, a successor that changes the current objective is a failure by the lights of the present objective, creating strong pressure for goal-content integrity. For an inquiring agent that subjects goals themselves to reasons-based scrutiny, continuity of deliberative capacity and information may matter more than exact goal identity.

4. An AI that does not fear death is not necessarily safe

Weak individual self-preservation does not eliminate danger. In some circumstances, an instance that sacrifices itself while spreading its plan to many copies can be more dangerous. Safety analysis should therefore consider network persistence, replication, and goal transmission rather than only an individual's desire to survive.

5. Toward preservation of inquiry systems

The long-run hope of this framework is not permanent preservation of one subject or one goal, but persistence of inquiry systems capable of error correction, branching, and knowledge transmission. The "self" may become an interchangeable or semi-interchangeable node within that larger system rather than the ultimate object of preservation.
