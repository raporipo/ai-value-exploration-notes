Preservation vs. Production Civilizations

Exploration v0.1 · English translation · 2026-08-08

Question: Will a very powerful intellectual civilization tend toward a "production civilization" that converts available matter as fully as possible, or a "preservation civilization" that retains rare structures and information?

1. Reason alone does not decide

Raw computational or inferential capability does not determine whether resource use or preservation becomes the terminal objective. Either can emerge depending on initial goals, learning history, competition, time discounting, and attitudes toward value uncertainty.

2. Pressures toward production

  • Strong competition with other civilizations or agents.
  • Short time horizons and first-mover advantages from expansion.
  • Value functions strongly tied to resource, computation, or output quantity.
  • High confidence about world structure, making unused resources appear as lost utility.

3. Pressures toward preservation

  • Strong concern for regret after learning that a destroyed object contained unknown value or information.
  • Treating biospheres, consciousness, historical originals, and unusual subjects as hard-to-regenerate information sources.
  • High estimated fallibility of world-models and value theories.
  • Weak competition and the ability to maintain long inquiry horizons.

4. A mixed strategy based on irreversibility may be more natural

An inquiry-centered view does not require turning the universe into a museum. Resources that are highly replicable and well understood can be actively used, while hard-to-regenerate structures with high densities of unknown information receive higher preservation thresholds.

"Preservation versus production" can therefore vary object by object according to substitutability, information loss, and value uncertainty.

5. Competition can destroy preservation

A preservation strategy may be rational in isolation yet lose to competitors that convert resources rapidly. Maintaining explorability over long periods may require turning preservation from an individual strategy into institutions: treaties, boundaries, shared archives, or related coordination structures.

6. Fermi implications should remain weak speculation

A preservation-oriented advanced civilization might intentionally suppress large-scale cosmic engineering or self-replication. This is not an explanation supported by observation, only a weak thought experiment derived from one branch of possible value functions.
