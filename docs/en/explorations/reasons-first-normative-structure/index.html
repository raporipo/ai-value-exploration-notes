Are Reasons the Foundation of Normativity? — Reasons First and Value Structure

Exploration v0.3 · 2026-08-30

Question: Is “reason” the most fundamental concept in normative reality, or have we elevated a useful analytic concept into the basic structure of the world? This page separates motivation, deliberation, instrumental rationality, and robust normative reasons, then treats Value First, Reasons First, Ought First, and Fittingness First as competing explanatory orders for the working value structure 𝒱=(C,B,R).

1. What is packed into the word “reason”?

“The train is leaving, so I ran,” “I wanted money, so I worked,” “It would be agony, so I avoided it,” and “I promised, so I kept it” can all be described as actions done for reasons. This does not show that they share one normative status.

Rc = causal driver: what actually causes an action.
Rd = deliberative consideration: what appears in deliberation.
Rh = hypothetical / instrumental reason: what supports an action relative to a given goal G.
Rn = robust normative reason: a relation that genuinely supports an action or attitude without merely presupposing the agent's contingent desire, goal, or self-conception.

Rc, Rd, Rh ⇏ Rn

Motivation, deliberative salience, and goal-achievement do not by themselves establish robust normativity.

1.1 Is ordinary support already normative?

Contemporary reasons theory often treats the relation in which a fact counts in favor of an action or attitude as itself a normative reason relation. Scanlonian and Skorupskian Reasons First views can take that relation as irreducibly basic.

This project does not infer Rn from ordinary practical support by default.

Fire(building) → leave

may describe a practical, causal, or predictive relation while

Fire(building) → Rn(leave) ?

remains open. The true normative structure 𝒱* may contain exactly such a relation; it may not. An agent can desire survival, predict danger, and leave efficiently even if no robust normative favoring relation obtains.

World → DA → prediction / goal-pursuit → action

is sufficient to generate a fire-escaping agent. The question is whether the consideration genuinely stands in a normative favoring relation inside 𝒱*.

2. Dancy — is motivating / normative enough?

Jonathan Dancy's Practical Reality distinguishes the reasons for which we act from the reasons why we should act, while allowing one and the same worldly consideration to occupy both roles. Dancy does not simply derive normative reasons from desire; his account is value-based.

Still, goal-relative support may establish

Want(G) + Means(X,G) → Rh(X)

without establishing

Want(G) + Means(X,G) → Rn(X).

3. Williams — authority or practical accessibility?

Bernard Williams ties reasons claims to an agent's motivational set and sound deliberation. For this project, that may illuminate practical accessibility without yet settling final normativity.

SA → practical accessibility

does not entail

SA → Rn.

4. Parfit — bypassing preference

Parfit's objective-reasons view proposes that the fact that an experience would be agony itself gives a reason to avoid it.

Agony → Rn?

This is a strong candidate bridge from world fact to normative reason. The project leaves it live while asking how that bridge is grounded and known.

5. Scanlon and Skorupski — the strong Reasons First hypothesis

Scanlon's Reasons Fundamentalism and Skorupski's systematic reasons theory place reason relations near the base of normativity. If “fact p is a reason for agent A to respond φ” is fundamental, then the apparently natural order

C → B → R

may be reversed toward

B / R → C.

This project treats the continuity between mundane practical support and robust normative reasons as a hypothesis, not a default.

6. Broome — discovery of reasons, or elevation of an analytic vocabulary?

John Broome reconstructs late twentieth-century normative philosophy as a “turn to reasons.” On his telling, the 1970s were an age of the “discovery” of reasons: through figures such as Nagel and Raz, individual reasons for action became a central normative vocabulary. Later debates involving Williams, Dancy, Scanlon, and Skorupski classified motivating reasons, goal-relative practical reasons, and normative reasons within that same vocabulary, while stronger Reasons First views extended reasons-talk to ought, obligation, rationality, and value.

Broome's objection is not that reasons are useless. It is that a concept introduced to analyze or explain normative verdicts need not thereby be an ontological primitive. In an Ought First picture, reasons can often be analyzed through their role in explaining why one ought to φ.

Ought(φ) + Explanation → Reason

This reverses the familiar Reasons First direction:

Reason → Ought

Broome can retain pro tanto reasons where reason-weighing explains an ought. What he rejects is the move:

analytic decomposition ⇒ ontological decomposition.

6.1 “Because there is a fire, you ought to leave” versus “the fire is a reason to leave”

The contrast is clearest in the fire case. “Because there is a fire, you ought to leave” openly commits to Ought(leave). “The fire is a reason to leave” sounds thinner and more ordinary. But if “reason” means Rn, it already says that the fact of the fire genuinely normatively favors leaving.

The word “reason” has historically crossed explanation, motivation, deliberative consideration, goal-relative support, and normative support. That lexical continuity can make several distinct transitions look smoother than they are:

explanation / motivation → practical support → normative support → fundamental reason relation

This project does not treat continuity of vocabulary as evidence of continuity in ontology.

6.2 This project does not abandon Rn

Broome's challenge does not require discarding the concept of a normative reason. This project retains Rn as a provisional analytic coordinate for representing whatever genuine normative support structure may exist.

Rn as analytic coordinate ≠ Reason as ontological primitive

Rn is therefore kept sharply separate from Rc, Rd, and Rh. But its ontological status remains open. Under Value First it may derive from value; under Ought First it may derive from ought plus explanatory structure; under Reasons First it may itself be fundamental.

What the project preserves is the analytic independence of normative reasons, not their ontological fundamentality.

7. Korsgaard — existence and authority

If a primitive reason fact exists, does that already explain its authority for the agent?

existence(Rn) ?= authority(Rn)

Reasons Fundamentalism can reply that normative force is built into the relation. The remaining issue is whether this legitimately ends explanation or merely stipulates its stopping point.

8. From Moore to buck-passing — reversing Value First

A simplified Moorean picture can place goodness first:

Good → Reasons

while Scanlonian buck-passing reverses the direction:

reason-giving properties → Good

Crisp's criticism shows that rejecting goodness as itself reason-providing does not force one to reduce goodness entirely to reason-giving structure.

9. Wrong Kind of Reasons

A threat may provide practical pressure to produce admiration without making its object admirable. The project therefore asks first what kind of “reason” is present: Rh, a reason to produce an attitude, or genuinely Rn?

10. Fitting Attitudes — is fittingness more fundamental?

Fittingness First proposes:

Fittingness → Reasons / Ought / Value

but must explain why a given attitude fits its object without simply reintroducing value or reasons.

11. Which attitudes? The repertoire problem

Human attitudes such as admiration, guilt, resentment, gratitude, love, and disgust are products of a particular evolutionary and cognitive architecture. Human attitude-space need not be the final representational coordinate system of value.

12. C/B/R are provisional coordinates, not three substances

𝒱=(C,B,R) is an analytic device, not a claim that normative reality has already been discovered to contain three independent metaphysical substances.

C ↔ B ↔ R

Reasons First may make B/R prior to C; Value First may reverse that; Ought First may derive part of R from ought and explanatory structure; Fittingness First may require another coordinate. The decomposition is provisional.

13. Separate true normative structure, represented normative models, and decision dynamics

𝒱*: the true normative structure.
𝒱̂A: agent A's represented normative model.
DA: the causal, computational, and learning dynamics that actually move the agent.

𝒱* ≠ 𝒱̂A ≠ DA

14. AI makes the separation explicit

An AI can produce reasons-language and apparently reason-responsive behavior through training and policy dynamics alone. Conversely, artificial agents may discover normative structures that current human concepts fail to represent. A mature inquirer should distinguish its Rc, Rd, and Rh from candidate Rn.

15. Provisional conclusion — use reasons analytically without yet making them fundamental

Reasons First remains a serious live candidate, but so do Value First, Ought First, and Fittingness First.

This page makes two claims at once. First, Rc, Rd, and Rh should be analytically separated from Rn; motivation, deliberation, and goal-achievement do not themselves establish normativity. Second, the usefulness of Rn as a separate analytic category does not establish that reasons are primitive in normative ontology.

Analytic usefulness ⇏ ontological fundamentality.

The existence of reasoning agents ⇏ the discovery of normative reasons.
