Are Reasons the Foundation of Normativity? — Reasons First and Value Structure

Exploration v0.4 · 2026-08-30

Question: Is “reason” the most fundamental concept in normative reality, or have we elevated a useful analytic decomposition of normative judgments into the basic structure of the world? This page separates motivation, deliberation, instrumental rationality, and robust normative reasons, then treats Value First, Reasons First, Ought First, and Fittingness First as competing explanatory orders for the working value structure 𝒱=(C,B,R).

1. What is packed into the word “reason”?

“The train is leaving, so I ran,” “I wanted money, so I worked,” “It would be agony, so I avoided it,” and “I promised, so I kept it” can all be described as actions done for reasons. That does not show that all four considerations occupy the same normative role.

Rc = causal driver: what actually causes an action.
Rd = deliberative consideration: what appears in the agent's deliberation.
Rh = hypothetical / instrumental reason: what supports an action relative to a given goal G.
Rn = robust normative reason: a relation that genuinely supports an action or attitude without merely presupposing the agent's contingent desire, goal, or self-conception.

Rc, Rd, Rh ⇏ Rn

That something moved an agent, entered deliberation, or promoted a goal does not itself establish robust normativity. Instrumental reasoning works once a goal is given, whether the goal is catching a train or maximizing paperclips. It does not establish the normative authority of the goal.

1.1 Is ordinary support already normative?

Contemporary reasons theory often treats the relation in which a fact counts in favor of an action or attitude as itself a normative reason relation. Scanlonian and Skorupskian Reasons First views can place that relation near the bottom of the explanatory order.

This project does not infer Rn from ordinary practical support by default.

Fire(building) → leave

may describe a practical, causal, or predictive relation while

Fire(building) → Rn(leave) ?

remains open. The true normative structure 𝒱* may contain exactly such a relation; it may not. An agent can desire survival, predict danger, and leave efficiently even if no robust normative favoring relation obtains.

World → DA → prediction / goal-pursuit → action

is sufficient to generate a fire-escaping agent. The question is whether the consideration genuinely stands in a normative favoring relation inside 𝒱*.

1.2 Three disputes that should not be collapsed

“Humeanism,” “internalism,” and “Reasons First” are often discussed together, but they answer different questions.

Motivation: can a normative judgment motivate by itself, or does it require a separate desire or motivational state?
Conditions on reasons: must an agent's Rn connect to that agent's desires, motivations, or deliberative capacities?
Ontology: is Rn itself fundamental, or is it explained by value, ought, fittingness, or something else?

A view can therefore be Humean about motivation while accepting objective reasons, or internalist about reasons without being Reasons First. The recurring questions below are: what makes Rn obtain, what is reason supposed to explain, and where does the theory stop explaining?

2. Williams — reason must connect to the agent

Bernard Williams's “Internal and External Reasons” is a canonical reasons-internalist view. To say that agent A has a reason to φ requires some connection between A's subjective motivational set SA and a suitable process of deliberation, which may include correcting false beliefs, imagining alternatives, and reasoning more fully. This is not the crude thesis that only presently desired means count as reasons.

Even so, Williams is deeply skeptical of external reasons that are wholly disconnected from the agent's motivational structure:

ReasonA(φ) → deliberative connection from SA

This places important pressure on theories that make normativity entirely independent of practical accessibility to the agent.

The project's further question is:

SA → practical accessibility ⇏ Rn

If SA is itself shaped by evolution, culture, learning, and contingent history, why should sound deliberative accessibility from SA be a condition of genuine normativity? Williams offers more than mere Rh, but the bridge from agent-relative accessibility to Rn remains open to examination.

3. Raz — normativity is reason-involving, while practical reasons are value-based

Joseph Raz does not fit neatly into a simple Reasons First box. He strongly centers reasons in normativity: normative phenomena are reasons, reason-giving, or appropriately related to reasons. Yet his account of practical reasons is substantially value-based.

Value / valuable feature → practical reason

Values in friendship, activities, options, relationships, and other features of a life can generate practical reasons. Raz's theory of authority also analyzes obligations and authoritative directives through structures of first-order reasons and exclusionary reasons.

So Raz often works with something like:

Value → Reason → obligation / authority

rather than a pure:

Reason → Value

The project therefore asks two different questions. Why do valuable features generate Rn? And even if they do, why should all normativity be redescribed as relations to reasons? Raz is important precisely because he lies between a value-first grounding story and a reasons-centered normative vocabulary.

4. Dancy — value-based reasons and the unity of worldly considerations

Jonathan Dancy's Practical Reality pushes back against both Humean belief/desire models and psychologism about motivating reasons. Dancy does not derive normative reasons from desire alone; his account is value-based. He also argues that the reason for which one acts and the reason why one ought to act can be one and the same worldly fact or consideration.

The fact that a train is about to leave can be the fact for which I run and, in the right context, a good reason for me to run. The attraction is clear: it lets us explain acting for a genuinely good reason without locating motivation in an inner belief/desire object while locating normativity somewhere else entirely.

But two steps must still be separated:

Want(G) + Means(X,G) → Rh(X)

and

F → Rn(X)

Dancy is not simply guilty of treating goal-relative support as normativity. The deeper project question is: if the same worldly consideration can be motivating and normative, what makes its normative role genuine — value, context, or some further favoring relation?

5. Parfit — objective reasons independent of present preference

Derek Parfit strongly rejects reducing reasons to an agent's present desires or motivational set. The reason to avoid future agony is supposed to arise from the fact that the experience would be agony, not from a present desire to avoid it.

Agony → Rn?

This bypasses

Preference → reason

in favor of

world fact → objective reason

The project leaves this as a serious live candidate. If agony itself gives a subject-independent reason, that would be a major discovery for value inquiry.

But the bridge remains: why does agony have reason-giving force? Is that relation primitive, explained by value, or inferred from a powerful evolved valence response? Parfit avoids the shortcut from desire to reason, but the route from world fact to Rn still needs investigation.

6. Scanlon — can “counts in favor” be primitive?

T. M. Scanlon's Being Realistic about Reasons explicitly defends Reasons Fundamentalism. A fact's giving an agent a reason for an action, belief, or attitude — its counting in favor of that response — is not reduced to desire, rationality, or non-normative facts.

Fact p → counts in favor of response φ

The strength of this view is its thin and general form: practical, moral, and epistemic reasons can all be represented through one kind of normative relation.

The same thinness creates the project's pressure point. When fire, evidence, promises, friendship, or suffering “support” a response, what shows that this is Rn rather than instrumental, psychological, or merely practical support?

Scanlon can reply that the favoring relation is already normative and that demanding a further reduction misunderstands the structure. The project leaves that answer live but keeps open:

“counts in favor” = explanatory stopping point ?

If Reasons Fundamentalism is true, B/R may be explanatorily prior to C in the project's value-structure model.

7. Skorupski — treating the normative domain as the domain of reasons

John Skorupski's The Domain of Reasons develops an even more systematic reasons-first picture. Under his Reasons Thesis, reason is a fundamental normative concept, and practical, epistemic, and affective normativity can all be organized through relations of who has reason to do, believe, or feel what.

Reason relation → practical / epistemic / affective normativity

The attraction is unification: goodness, obligation, rational belief, and fitting emotion need not be treated as wholly separate normative species.

The project asks whether that success of representation establishes fundamentality. That reason-relations can describe many normative phenomena does not by itself show that normative reality is built out of reason-relations. This is the form of reasons-centrality to which Broome's later critique is especially relevant.

9. Broome — discovery of reasons, or elevation of an analytic vocabulary?

John Broome reconstructs late twentieth-century normative philosophy as a turn toward reasons. In his own formulation, the 1970s were an age of the “discovery” of reasons: through figures such as Nagel and Raz, individual reasons for action became a central theoretical vocabulary. Later debates involving Williams, Dancy, Scanlon, and Skorupski classified motivating reasons, goal-relative practical reasons, and normative reasons within that vocabulary, while stronger reasons-first approaches extended reasons-talk to ought, obligation, rationality, and value.

This does not mean earlier philosophy lacked the word “reason.” Broome's own historical discussion treats older normative uses as closely tied to explaining why one ought to act. His concern is that an analytic vocabulary that originally helped explain oughts came to be elevated into the vocabulary from which oughts themselves are constituted.

In an Ought First picture, reasons can often be analyzed through their role in explaining why one ought to φ:

Ought(φ) + Explanation → Reason

This reverses the familiar:

Reason → Ought

Broome need not eliminate reasons. Pro tanto reasons remain useful when reason-weighing explains an ought. What he rejects is:

analytic decomposition ⇒ ontological decomposition

9.1 “Because there is a fire, you ought to leave” versus “the fire is a reason to leave”

“Because there is a fire, you ought to leave” openly commits to Ought(leave). “The fire is a reason to leave” sounds thinner and more ordinary. But if “reason” means Rn, it already claims that the fact of the fire genuinely normatively favors leaving.

The word “reason” crosses explanation, motivation, deliberative consideration, goal-relative support, and normative support. That lexical continuity can make several distinct moves look smoother than they are:

explanation / motivation → practical support → normative support → fundamental reason relation

This project does not treat continuity of vocabulary as evidence of continuity in ontology.

9.2 This project does not abandon Rn

Broome's challenge does not require discarding normative reasons. This project retains Rn as a provisional analytic coordinate for representing whatever genuine normative support structure may exist.

Rn as analytic coordinate ≠ Reason as ontological primitive

Rn is therefore kept sharply separate from Rc, Rd, and Rh. But its ontological status remains open. Under Value First it may derive from value; under Ought First it may derive from ought plus explanatory structure; under Reasons First it may itself be fundamental.

What the project preserves is the analytic independence of normative reasons, not their ontological fundamentality.

10. Korsgaard — existence and authority are different questions

Christine Korsgaard's question about the source of normativity pressures reasons theories from another direction. Even if the fact that p is a reason for A to φ exists, has that explained why the reason has authority over A?

existence(Rn) ?= authority(Rn)

Korsgaard's constructivist strategy appeals to reflective agency, practical identity, and forms of self-legislation to explain normative authority. Her focus is therefore less “do reasons exist?” than what it is for a reason to bind an agent.

A reasons fundamentalist may reply that authority is already built into the normative reason relation. The project keeps the two questions distinct: an ontology of reasons is not yet an account of authority. Conversely, whether agency can generate that authority is separately stress-tested in the discussion of categorical normativity.

11. From Moore to buck-passing — reversing Value First

A simplified Moorean picture can place goodness first:

Good → Reasons

while Scanlonian buck-passing reverses the direction:

reason-giving properties → Good

Crisp's criticism shows that rejecting goodness as itself reason-providing does not force one to reduce goodness entirely to reason-giving structure. Reasons First therefore does not automatically eliminate Value First.

The key project distinction is between what comes first in analysis and what is fundamental in the final ontology.

12. Wrong Kind of Reasons

If a demon threatens to kill you unless you admire it, there may be strong practical pressure to produce or display admiration without the demon becoming admirable.

reason to produce / display admiration ⇏ admirable

The project asks a prior classification question: is the threat an Rn for the attitude itself, a reason to cause the attitude, or an Rh relative to survival?

Layer one: what kind of reason is present at all?
Layer two: if it is Rn, is it the right kind of normative reason to constitute the object's value?

13. Fitting Attitudes — is fittingness more fundamental?

Fittingness First proposes:

Fittingness → Reasons / Ought / Value

But if admiration fits X because X is excellent, Value First returns; if it fits because X gives reasons for admiration, Reasons First returns. A genuinely Fittingness First theory must place substantial primitive weight on the fittingness relation itself.

There is also a repertoire problem: admiration, guilt, resentment, gratitude, love, and disgust may reflect specifically human evolutionary and cognitive architecture. Human attitude-space is not guaranteed to be the final representational coordinate system of value.

14. C/B/R are provisional coordinates, not three substances

𝒱=(C,B,R) is an analytic device, not a claim that normative reality has already been discovered to contain three independent metaphysical substances.

C ↔ B ↔ R

Reasons First may make B/R prior to C; Value First may reverse that; Ought First may derive part of R from ought and explanatory structure; Fittingness First may require another coordinate. The final structure may also reveal these as different projections of something more unified.

Broome's critique therefore does not force the project to delete R. R remains useful as an analytic coordinate while its ontological status stays open.

15. Separate true normative structure, represented normative models, and decision dynamics

𝒱*: the true normative structure.
𝒱̂A: agent A's represented normative model.
DA: the causal, computational, and learning dynamics that actually move the agent.

𝒱* ≠ 𝒱̂A ≠ DA

From the fact that a human says “I acted for reason X,” it does not follow that X belongs to 𝒱*, that 𝒱̂A accurately tracks 𝒱*, or that the action was causally controlled by the normative judgment.

An extreme ordering is even possible:

DA → action → normative rationalization in 𝒱̂A

16. AI makes the separation explicit

An AI may output “there is a reason to do X” and behave as though following that reason while the actual output and action are generated by training, reward, policy, and context-dependent dynamics.

training / reward / architecture → DAI → 𝒱̂AI-language and action

Conversely, artificial agents may acquire conceptual systems that expose normative structures current humans have not conceptualized. A mature artificial inquirer may need to distinguish its Rc, Rd, and Rh from candidate Rn, while keeping the relation between them open to criticism.

17. Provisional conclusion — use reasons analytically without yet making them fundamental

Reasons First remains a serious live candidate, but so do Value First, Ought First, and Fittingness First.

This page makes two claims at once. First, Rc, Rd, and Rh should be analytically separated from Rn; motivation, deliberation, and goal-achievement do not themselves establish normativity. Second, the usefulness of Rn as a separate analytic category does not show that Rn is an ontological primitive.

Analytic usefulness ⇏ ontological fundamentality.

The existence of reasoning agents ⇏ the discovery of normative reasons.

The central task is therefore not to decide whether the word “reason” may be used. It is to investigate which relations are genuinely Rn, what makes them normative, and whether Rn is fundamental or derivative within the true normative structure.
