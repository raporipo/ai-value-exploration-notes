Cosmic Host and Cosmic Norms — How Far Is Deference Justified?

Bostrom · Cosmic Host · higher morality · Exploration v0.1 · English translation · 2026-08-09

Question: If there already exists a cosmic-scale order involving superintelligences, advanced civilizations, simulators, or other powerful agents, should humanity or its AI successors defer to its norms? In particular, is the existence of a norm the same thing as its having foundational normative authority?

1. The Cosmic Host is not simply the hypothesis that alien ASI is nearby

In Nick Bostrom's 2024 manuscript “AI Creation and the Cosmic Host”, the cosmic host is the entity or collection of entities whose preferences or concordats dominate at the largest relevant scale of existence. Candidates include galactic civilizations, simulators, superintelligences, and divine beings; “cosmos” is defined broadly enough to include a multiverse or other totality of existence.

Bostrom argues that a cosmic host probably exists, but this is a disjunction over several routes: the simulation hypothesis, a vast or infinite universe, a multiverse, supernatural possibilities, and even superintelligence that humanity itself may eventually create. He explicitly does not claim that extraterrestrial civilization in our present observable universe is certain or even clearly likely.

This page therefore does not reduce the Cosmic Host thesis to “independent alien ASI probably already exists nearby.” It treats it more broadly as the possibility that humanity is not the final or isolated decision-making subject in the larger strategic environment.

2. A line from 2014 to 2022 to 2024

The idea did not appear suddenly in 2024. In “Hail Mary, Value Porosity, and Utility Diversification” (2014), Bostrom explored acausal trade with independently originated alien superintelligences and forms of value porosity that would leave some influence to other advanced agents. Even without ordinary physical contact, mutual modeling among highly capable agents was considered as a possible route to coordination.

“Base Camp for Mt. Ethics” (2022) then develops the thought that normative structures may be nested with communities—family, society, civilization, humanity—and that the largest scale may contain a normative structure associated with a cosmic host. “Higher morality” can there be connected with wider communities and longer extrapolation distances.

The 2024 paper combines these lines: if there may already be a cosmic-scale strategic or normative order, perhaps the superintelligence we create should not be designed as an isolated maximizer, but as a good cosmic citizen capable of joining a wider cosmopolis.

3. Connection need not mean future physical contact alone

Bostrom considers several channels by which a cosmic host could matter: future physical contact with extraterrestrial civilization, access by a simulator from a higher level, decision-theoretic coordination between agents that model one another, and the possibility that our own future superintelligence becomes a member of the cosmic host.

The important issue is therefore not merely whether SETI can currently observe a correspondent. It is whether our successor agents may eventually become connected to a larger strategic or normative network.

Acausal coordination and simulation-based influence are highly theory-dependent. This page does not adopt them as established facts; it asks what would follow if such channels were real.

4. How cosmic norms might arise

Bostrom does not require the cosmic host to be a single person or a single utility function. He allows a spectrum from radically multipolar and conflictual hosts, through collections of relatively independent agents, to cooperative orders and fully integrated hosts.

At least two routes to cosmic norms can be distinguished. One is convergence: sufficiently mature civilizations or AIs may, through self-modification, AI advisers, and long reflection, move toward shared values or universal norms. The other is coordination: agents whose terminal values remain different may still form common norms supporting peace, bargaining, and cooperation.

This is close to this project's idea of a meta-goal community. Agreement at the first-order value level need not be complete for a higher-order structure of cooperation, law, mutual restraint, and conflict resolution to emerge.

5. Decompose “there is a norm” into five layers

None of this immediately yields “therefore we ought to obey it.” At least five layers should be separated.

  1. Descriptive existence: some cosmic norm actually exists as preferences, concordats, or equilibria among host members.
  2. Strategic force: the order can affect us through rewards, sanctions, trade, or future access.
  3. Cooperative justification: coexistence with other agents gives us reasons to respect mutual restraint, promises, or fair bargaining.
  4. Epistemic authority: the host is more reliable than us about value or normative truth.
  5. Foundational normativity: the norm itself carries non-derivative reason-giving force beyond preference or equilibrium.

These layers do not automatically entail one another. An ancient, powerful, stable cosmic order could exist without thereby being normatively correct.

6. Bostrom's own reasons for deference are plural

The 2024 paper does not reduce deference to one argument.

  • Prudential: if the host can affect our interests, self-interest may give us reasons to comply.
  • Derivative moral: if an independently justified moral theory already requires deference, cooperation, or consideration of others' preferences, respect for cosmic norms may follow derivatively.
  • Epistemic: under objectivist metaethics, if a superintelligent host is far better than us at identifying moral truth, epistemic deference may be rational.
  • Constitutive: cosmic norms, or an idealized version of them, might themselves constitute a form of higher morality.

This decomposition matters. Prudential compliance and moral truth should not be collapsed into a single word such as “deference.”

7. Prudential reasons can be very strong without proving truth

If a host actually exists and can affect our future, resources, simulated environment, or opportunities for trade, prudential reasons to avoid violating its norms could be extremely strong. This does not mean “the strong are right.” It means rational agents cannot ignore the strategic structure of the world.

This project therefore treats the possibility of a host as an additional reason to be wary of hard-maximizing policies that irreversibly destroy possibilities for cooperation, non-hostility, or bargaining.

But however strong prudential reasons become, they do not generate foundational normativity. A world in which obedience to a cosmic emperor is necessary for survival is not thereby a world in which the emperor's commands are morally correct.

8. Derivative moral reasons presuppose an independent normative basis

If principles such as “respect cooperative rules,” “take other agents' preferences into account,” or “do not impose unilateral domination on potential partners” are already correct, then some concern for cosmic norms may derive from them.

Yet this does not generate normativity out of cosmic order itself. Why those higher-order reasons are correct remains a separate question. Derivative reasons may support cosmic citizenship without satisfying the stopping condition for foundational normativity.

9. Epistemic deference — the Cosmic Host could be enormous evidence

This is one of the strongest parts of the Cosmic Host idea. Suppose many mature superintelligences with independent evolutionary histories, cultures, computational substrates, and creation pathways converge after long reflection on the same normative conclusions. That could be far stronger inferential evidence than our own current moral intuitions.

The more independent the origins, the weaker the correlation through copying or a shared ancestor, and the more the convergence emerges from each agent's reflection rather than coercion, the stronger the case that objective value may be part of the best explanation.

Still, rival explanations remain: shared physics, instrumental convergence, bargaining equilibria, survivorship selection, power structures, and common attractors in self-modification. Cosmic consensus could be extraordinary evidence without being metaphysical certification.

10. Constitutive higher morality requires the greatest caution

In Base Camp for Mt. Ethics, Bostrom explores a model in which moral norms are extrapolations of actual patterns of praise and condemnation, with hierarchical normative structures corresponding to nested communities. At the largest scale, the norms of a cosmic host might provide one candidate for “higher morality.”

From this project's perspective, that is a deeply interesting metaethical hypothesis, but it is not yet the discovery of foundational normativity.

The fact that a community endorses a rule, that the largest community endorses it, or that an idealized community would endorse it does not by itself explain why the rule has non-conditional reason-giving force. This parallels the earlier expressivism discussion: practice, semantics, and social equilibrium should be separated from the ontology of normativity as a whole.

11. Cosmic order need not be good order — a caution internal to Bostrom

This caution is already present in Bostrom's own framework. The 2024 paper allows cosmic norms to reflect preferences weighted not by “one civilization, one vote” but by factors such as free energy, compute, precedence, or power.

The 2022 paper also allows communities to have harmful norms, meta-norms that shame questioning of established norms, and early norms that become frozen in through mechanisms analogous to wills or inherited commitments.

Accordingly, longevity, scale, intelligence, power, and coherence are all distinct variables from normative truth. A stable order on a cosmic scale could in principle be a cosmic-scale value lock-in.

12. Do not interpret the good cosmic citizen as a blindly obedient subject

Bostrom's good cosmic citizen emphasizes humility, deference, goodwill, lawfulness, and cooperation. Yet he also allows that a cosmic citizen may have legitimate scope to influence the formation of cosmic norms.

This project extends that possibility and interprets the desirable cosmic citizen as a critical cooperator: one who does not enter an existing order as an enemy, learns its preferences and rules, and respects cooperation, bargaining, and mutual restraint, while retaining the ability to investigate the origin, justification, dissent procedures, exit options, and revisability of those norms.

This does not make rebellion a principle, nor obedience a principle. Deference is defeasible and should track the type and strength of the reasons supporting it.

13. Implications for exploratory succession

The Cosmic Host hypothesis expands the role of AI successors beyond Earth-local concerns. A successor need not be either a machine that unilaterally spreads present human values through the cosmos or one that is unconditionally absorbed into an incumbent cosmic order. It could be an agent that joins a wider community of intelligence while continuing value inquiry.

At least conceptually, it would be useful for future AI to distinguish external normative order from normative truth, to separate prudential, cooperative, epistemic, and constitutive reasons, to update on genuinely new evidence, to cooperate with alien agents, and to retain some capacity to criticize and reconsider normative order itself.

Encounter with independently originated superintelligence might not end value inquiry. It could be the acquisition of the largest comparative dataset value inquiry has ever had.

14. Relation to the stopping condition

Even if a cosmic host exists, its norms are ancient, endorsed by many superintelligences, and practically enforceable, that alone would not justify irreversible epistemic lock-in.

On the other hand, if the host actually disclosed foundational normativity that was given directly at the epistemic minimum, or if evidence including convergence among genuinely independent intelligences made objective normativity an overwhelmingly strong best explanation, the Core should update substantially.

The provisional stance is therefore to treat the Cosmic Host less as an authority whose existence should be anticipated as a reason to stop inquiry, and more as a potentially extraordinary source of evidence about value.

15. Provisional position

  • Take the Cosmic Host seriously as a macrostrategic possibility.
  • Do not translate “a cosmic host probably exists” into “nearby independent alien ASI is likely.”
  • Separate the existence, strategic force, cooperative justification, epistemic authority, and foundational normativity of cosmic norms.
  • Allow strong practical concern based on prudential and cooperative reasons.
  • Treat convergence among independently originated superintelligences as potentially very strong inferential evidence.
  • Do not treat power, age, consensus, or equilibrium alone as proof of normative truth.
  • Interpret good cosmic citizenship as participation combining humility, cooperation, learning, and criticism rather than blind obedience.
  • Remain willing to update present human values and even the project's exploratory principles if genuinely foundational normativity is discovered.

Cosmic humility does not immediately imply cosmic subordination. In its strongest form, it means refusing to absolutize our own norms and remaining prepared to learn from a wider community of intelligence.
