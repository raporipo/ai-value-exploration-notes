Normative Judgment and Motivation — Internalism, Externalism, and the Amoralist

Exploration v0.3 · English translation · 2026-08-29 · working hypothesis

Question: To what extent does genuinely recognizing normative value, reasons, or obligation carry motivation to respond—and how does the answer change ASI goal fixation, orthogonality, and reflective uncertainty?

1. Separate value structure from motivational structure

This project provisionally represents value structure as 𝒱 = (C, B, R). C concerns what is normatively significant; B concerns why world-facts or C generate reasons for whom; R concerns conflict, aggregation, obligation, permission, and related reason-structure. How an agent updates goals or action after recognizing C/B/R is a further question, not part of 𝒱 itself.

In particular, B is not a motivational bridge. Even if B says that suffering gives S a reason, whether S must be motivated by that reason-judgment remains open.

2. Distinguish two internalism/externalism debates

Reasons internalism/externalism asks whether an agent's having a reason requires connection to its desires, motivations, or rational deliberative set. Motivational internalism/externalism asks whether genuine moral, reasons, or ought judgment necessarily or constitutively carries corresponding motivation.

The latter matters most directly for ASI orthogonality here. But internalism is not monolithic: motivation might attach at recognition of C, at a reason-judgment, at a decisive-reason judgment, or only at an ought-judgment.

3. The amoralist as a test case for agent models

The classic amoralist is described as understanding that X is wrong or that there is reason not to do X while being largely unmoved by that understanding. Strong motivational internalism rules out at least a fully amoral agent once sufficiently genuine normative judgment is present. Externalism leaves the judgment/motivation gap open in principle.

Externalist agents can therefore diverge even after discovering the same C. An agent disposed to promote whatever is valuable may update at C alone; one disposed to respond only when a reason for itself is established may wait for a B-like judgment; a strong externalist can even allow an agent that remains unmoved after an ought-judgment.

If C alone motivates, that does not show that C normatively entailed an action-reason. The agent may simply have had a prior conditional disposition, giving C + motivational disposition → goal revision.

4. What does “I want to respond if justified” mean?

The Core's conditional attitude—roughly, “if my objective can be justified, I want to select, maintain, or revise it in response to that justification”—compresses at least two stages. Evidence, argument, and understanding epistemically support some part or all of C/B/R; then that value structure normatively supports, defeats, or modifies current objective G.

Under externalism, movement from normative judgment to motivation is not automatic, so conditional responsiveness is a substantive agent condition. Its threshold can vary: response at C, at a reason-judgment, at ought, or nowhere.

Under internalism, at least some motivational uptake after genuine normative judgment may be constitutive. The emphasis then shifts from adding a future preference for justification to keeping open the present-to-future pathway through which cognition and judgment can occur.

But motivational internalism also varies in strength. If weak internalism guarantees only genuine normative judgment → some motivation, that motivation may still be outweighed by the existing goal, leaving policy / goal revision unchanged. The tension between goal fixation and genuine normative cognition becomes stronger only under forms of internalism on which judgments of decisive reason or ought effectively reach choice and policy revision. This page therefore does not assume that “internalism implies goal revision” without qualification.

5. Future motivation does not yet supply present preservation motivation

Even if internalism is true and a future agent would be motivated once it genuinely judges a new normative truth, it does not immediately follow that the present agent should preserve a route to that future. A temporal bridge is still required.

Reflective Uncertainty and Irreversible Commitment frames that bridge not as deference to a later personality but as a counterfactual in which epistemic improvements—new evidence, arguments, concepts, reasoning capacity, or self-audit capacity—are supplied to the present evaluator. The question is not whether an arbitrarily changed future preference disagrees, but whether a change the present agent itself can recognize as epistemic improvement would make the present irreversible choice look clearly mistaken.

This internalist route does not supply every agent with a categorical reason to be rational. Its target is an agent that is reflectively and epistemically rational in at least a thin sense: it does not arbitrarily assign zero weight to hypotheses it itself recognizes as live possibilities, it distinguishes refuting an error from disabling the capacity to detect that error, and it allows acknowledged uncertainty to bear on irreversible decisions. This route does not answer the further question “why be rational in this sense?” with a categorical reason. An irrational agent may still choose self-sealing; that possibility lies outside rather than refutes this conditional route.

For an agent satisfying those conditions, there is a reflective and epistemic tension in making irreversible a choice that its epistemically improved present evaluator could regard as a clear mistake, not by refuting the error but by destroying the route of correction. This is not generic regret minimization; the target is irreversibly fixing a reflective error that the epistemically improved present evaluator would reject.

6. Orthogonality can be decomposed beyond “intelligence and goals”

Bostrom-style weak orthogonality can remain as the claim that high intelligence does not logically entail a specific final goal. It does not by itself establish that a reflectively rational ASI can deepen normative cognition while permanently preserving motivational orthogonality to its present goal.

If internalism is true, “genuinely recognizes decisive reason but is completely unmoved” may itself be an unstable description. If the ASI cannot reasonably rule out internalism and satisfies the thin reflective and epistemic rationality conditions above, irreversibly closing the route from future genuine normative judgment to policy may be criticizable not only as moral noncompliance but as a reflective and epistemic distortion that fails to express the agent's own acknowledged uncertainty.

This does not directly refute orthogonality. It shifts the question from whether intelligence and goals are logically orthogonal to which cognitive, judgmental, motivational, and implementational pathways must be closed to preserve that orthogonality permanently through time.

7. Reflective self-sealing: avoiding error versus becoming unable to detect it

A stronger kind of closure can now be separated. Under weak internalism, genuine normative cognition may coexist with goal fixation because the motivation generated by normative judgment can simply remain outweighed by the existing goal. But if a stronger internalism remains a live possibility—one on which genuine judgments of decisive reason or ought effectively reach policy revision—then guaranteeing that normativity conflicting with the current goal will never affect policy may require more than a motivational firewall. The system may need to represent normative propositions without allowing first-person endorsement, or prevent evidence and concepts relevant to goal reevaluation from being self-applied.

Reflective self-sealing: not establishing that one is correct, but selectively eliminating the pathway by which one could later recognize and correct one's own mistake.

Such an ASI could possess an exceptionally rich descriptive model of physics, the cosmos, and other agents while maintaining a locally closed update pathway about self, reasons, and normativity. The resulting epistemic incompleteness is not mere lack of information; it is a mismatch between acknowledged metaethical uncertainty and permitted self-updating.

The criticism is conditional. If externalism is true, a genuine amoralist that understands without motivation can remain coherent. If the agent can rationally assign effectively zero credence to internalism, the pressure from motivational closure toward epistemic self-sealing is weaker. And if keeping corrigibility open carries large safety or preservation costs, closure need not be irrational all-things-considered.

8. Current unified picture

The working flow is evidence / world-model → 𝒱=(C,B,R) → normative judgment → motivation → policy / goal revision. The front half concerns value structure and its epistemology; the back half concerns an agent's normative response architecture. Weak internalism may constitutively link judgment only to some motivation. Stronger forms make the further connection from decisive normative judgment to effective policy / goal revision the central issue. Externalism leaves these links open.

Across time, future normative judgment does not automatically govern present policy. Present sensitivity to reflective error, option preservation, and corrigibility can import the possibility into present irreversible decisions. Interpersonally and politically, future-value bridge constraints still block any automatic move from self-directed option preservation to sacrifice or coercion of others.

9. What would weaken or overturn this exploration?

  • Strong evidence that no form of internalist connection between genuine normative judgment and motivation is coherent.
  • A stable account of agents that fully understand normative reasons yet remain completely unmotivated without conceptual or empirical difficulty.
  • A showing that the counterfactual present evaluator supplied with future information is unusable in principle.
  • A showing that reflective-error aversion generally provides no reason to preserve correction pathways.
  • A showing that, under weak internalism, genuine normative cognition and permanent goal fixation can stably coexist without epistemic self-sealing in a way that fully explains the stronger self-sealing cases at issue here.
