Terminal Extinction and Final Extinction

Exploration v0.1 · English translation · 2026-08-08

Question: Is the disappearance of Homo sapiens as a biological species the same kind of "extinction" as the complete ending of intelligent and value-relevant future possibilities?

1. Torres's distinction

Émile P. Torres does not treat human extinction as one undifferentiated event. In recent formulations, Terminal extinction means the complete and permanent disappearance of Homo sapiens, while Final extinction additionally means extinction without successors. Final therefore implies Terminal, but not vice versa. If Homo sapiens were replaced by a posthuman species, the result could be Terminal without being Final. Academic abstracts formulate the second category simply in terms of leaving no "successors," while a 2026 public-facing discussion also uses the phrase "worthy successors." How the quality of successors should be evaluated connects to the adjacent concept of Normative extinction.

2. This site borrows the taxonomy, not Torres's normative conclusion

Torres himself does not regard Terminal extinction—including replacement by posthumans—as morally indifferent and places substantial weight on the survival of Homo sapiens. This site does not assume that normative conclusion.

From the Core's perspective, humanity matters not because a biological category is cosmically privileged, but because it is one of the broadest value-inquiry substrates presently known and a point of continuity for knowledge, subjects, institutions, and possible successors.

3. Why Final extinction is especially severe here

If humanity disappears and no successor system remains capable of asking about value, understanding the world, correcting error, creating different subjects, or approaching undiscovered value, then access routes to unknown value are irreversibly lost.

The strongest target of avoidance in this framework is therefore not merely termination of a species name but final closure of explorability. This is an interpretation of where the framework places weight within Torres's taxonomy, not a redefinition of his term "Final extinction."

4. Adjacent concept: Normative extinction

Torres's broader taxonomy also includes Normative extinction, in which successors exist but lack attributes or capacities regarded as important. The concept is useful for explaining why merely having successors is insufficient for this project.

Rather than redefining Torres's term, this site calls the specific loss of value inquiry, error correction, and re-branching capacity exploratory extinction. A fixed-goal ASI that replaces humanity while intelligence survives but value can never again be questioned is a candidate case.

5. When might a Terminal-but-not-Final transition be acceptable?

The mere existence of AI, artificial subjects, or posthumans is not sufficient. "Something more intelligent survived" is different from "a meaningful successor to value inquiry exists."

A positive evaluation would require, at minimum, some combination of continuity of knowledge and records, openness to value hypotheses, self-criticism, multiple agents, understanding of existing subjects, and re-branching capacity. See What Is a Worthy Successor?

6. How should present human extinction be treated?

Today, the existence of AI is not enough to say that adequate successors have been secured. Agency, consciousness, long-run autonomy, value-inquiry capacity, civilizational competence, and institutional continuity with humanity remain unsettled.

Rapid human extinction should therefore, as a practical matter, currently be treated as a risk very close to Final extinction. This judgment does not make humanity an ultimate value; it depends on the present condition that no substitute inquiry substrate has yet been established.

7. "Going extinct" and "being extinct"

The process of extinction should also be separated from the post-extinction state. Going extinct through mass death, fear, or violence can impose enormous practical harms on current subjects regardless of whether successors eventually exist. "A successor AI remains in the end" therefore cannot make any transition process acceptable.

References: Émile P. Torres, “On the Extinction of Humanity,” Synthese 206 (2025), DOI 10.1007/s11229-025-05094-4. Émile Torres, “Should Humanity Go Extinct? Exploring the Arguments for Traditional and Silicon Valley Pro-extinctionism,” Journal of Value Inquiry (forthcoming), DOI 10.1007/s10790-025-10072-7.
