Population Ethics and Future Subjects

Exploration v0.2 · English translation · 2026-08-08

Question: In a future containing humans, AI, artificial consciousness, and modified subjects, how does the decision of whom to bring into existence change value inquiry itself?

The bearers of value cannot be fixed in advance

Population ethics concerns not only the welfare of existing subjects but which future subjects should exist. In the AI era, the types of subject themselves may become design variables, making the problem more radical.

If subjects with different cognitive structures or phenomenal experience can detect value-relevant facts inaccessible to present humans, subject creation becomes not only a question of population size but also a design problem for the exploration space.

New subjects may change the epistemic layer of value cognition

Even if objective normativity is not directly given to current humans at the epistemic minimum, it need not be impossible for every possible subject. Artificial or modified subjects might possess different phenomenal or inferential structures that open new epistemic routes to value.

Subject diversity could therefore matter not merely as diversity of values but as exploration of different ways of recognizing value. This does not justify unconstrained subject creation.

Danger

The slogan "create diverse subjects to expand inquiry" can license the creation of suffering subjects or the instrumentalization of persons for research. Protecting agentic explorability does not by itself justify how newly created subjects are treated.

Open problems

  • How far can concern for potential subjects apply before they exist?
  • Under what conditions, if any, may artificial consciousness be created as a means of inquiry?
  • How should the number of subjects be distinguished from diversity in kinds of subject?
