Foundational thesis

The Limits of Inferential Certainty About Value

Working draft v0.4 · English translation · 2026-08-08

Thesis: Inference can compare value hypotheses and reach powerful best explanations of the world. This framework therefore does not deny that objective value might be discovered through inference with extremely high confidence. But insofar as the inferential system and world-model remain fallible, inferential value—even when it warrants strong practical commitment—should be distinguished from an absolute foundation that licenses the irreversible destruction of every route for reconsideration.

1. The question is not whether value can be inferred

If value premises are included, further value claims can be inferred; and descriptive evidence about the world could in principle make moral realism the best explanation. This thesis does not claim that inference tells us nothing about value.

The relevant distinction is between very high confidence reached by inference and an absolute foundation not mediated by inference.

2. Inference cannot completely certify itself from outside itself

If the validity of an inferential rule R is proved by another inference, that proof itself uses some inferential rule. If it uses R or an equivalent rule, there is circularity; if it uses a different rule R2, the validity of R2 becomes the next question.

This is not an argument that inference should be distrusted. Coherence, predictive success, compression, cross-checking, and successful intervention can justify overwhelming rational confidence in an inferential system. The point is that this confidence remains inside the inferential web.

3. At least two routes to value cognition are conceivable

Foundational presentation: a hypothetical case in which value with normative force—beyond mere pleasure, pain, or preference—is directly given with epistemic strength comparable to the minimum foundation.

Inferential discovery: a case in which, after integrating evidence about consciousness, subjects, rationality, or cosmic structure, a theory positing objective value overwhelmingly outperforms its rivals as the best explanation.

Most forms of moral realism we can realistically investigate today belong to the second route. That does not make them weak. Like belief in the physical world, an inferential best explanation can in principle become so strong that it is treated as practically near-certain.

4. There are multiple bridges from phenomenal value to normativity

  1. There is an appearance of pain.
  2. The subject has negative valence or an avoidance tendency toward it.
  3. Other subjects have structurally similar appearances.
  4. There is reason to treat those subjects symmetrically across differences of identity.
  5. That symmetry is not merely a consistency convention but has universal normative force.
  6. Therefore rational agents have reason to reduce that pain.

These are not one proposition. In particular, the appearance of "badness" or aversion is not automatically identical to the appearance of a subject-independent ought.

5. The still-unknown structure of the world can matter to value inference

We need not say that no ethical judgment is possible until the entire universe is understood. But many ontological, consciousness-related, subject-related, and cosmological facts that could affect the truth conditions of value remain deeply unsettled.

The structure of consciousness, personal identity, treatment of copied subjects, artificial consciousness, an infinite universe, or the relation between experience and normativity could all change the meaning or consequences of the same ethical theory. A future theory of objective value should therefore, insofar as it remains at the inferential layer, be understood as a best explanation conditional on the world-model then available.

6. A best explanation can bind practice very strongly

Fallibility does not entail weak action. If one value theory overwhelms its competitors in explanatory power, prediction, and coherence, institutions and resource allocation may rationally shift very strongly toward it.

The proposed asymmetry is to let the strength of practical commitment rise with the evidence, while imposing a distinct threshold for irreversible closure of inquiry. Confidence of 99.9999% may justify very strong action; it does not automatically justify physically destroying every route by which the residual uncertainty could ever be examined.

7. Why skepticism can remain even when inferential value is strong

Skepticism here does not mean refusing to adopt a conclusion. It means adopting the current best explanation while retaining its error conditions. For an inferential theory of value, one keeps track of what could falsify it, which changes in the world-model would alter the conclusion, and how it might appear under different structures of agency.

In this sense, appropriate skepticism is not the enemy of value realization. It is an error-correction mechanism in case the theory of value is wrong.

8. The asymmetry of normative stakes

If objective value does not exist, there is—objectively—no value difference between inquiry and non-inquiry. If objective value does exist, however, there may be unknown and extremely consequential normative differences. The nihilist hypothesis therefore does not itself provide an objective reason that inquiry ought to stop.

To connect this asymmetry to action, however, one still needs a conditional meta-attitude: if true value exists, I do not want to be permanently indifferent to it. This thesis does not hide that additional commitment.

9. A correction to the simple slogan "uncertainty implies inquiry"

In an earlier, weaker formulation, corrigibility, information value, and avoidance of irreversible loss had to be introduced as independent meta-preferences. The revised view still needs them, but gives them a narrower role.

Preserving explorability is not a substitute for value itself. It is an error-correction strategy adopted by an agent that is not indifferent to true value while the epistemic status of value remains inferential. If value were ever absolutely given at the foundational layer, this strategy could itself be withdrawn.

10. Implications for ASI

A superintelligence might understand the physical world, its own formation, human evolutionary history, and every known ethical theory without thereby receiving foundational normativity directly. At the same time, it might use vastly stronger inference than humans to discover objective value as the best explanation.

Two roles for ASI therefore remain open: first, drastically raising our inferential confidence in value theories; second, opening modes of cognition unavailable to humans and testing whether foundational normativity exists at all.

11. What would weaken this thesis?

  • A demonstration that phenomenal value analytically or directly contains subject-independent normativity.
  • An epistemic route that can validate inference from outside the inferential web without itself relying on inference.
  • A complete determination of every aspect of world structure relevant to the truth conditions of value, leaving only one value theory as necessary.
  • A demonstration that the distinction between inferential best explanation and absolute foundation has no meaningful application to value cognition.
