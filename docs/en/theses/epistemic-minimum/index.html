Foundational thesis

The Epistemic Minimum: Momentary Appearance

Working draft v0.3 · English translation · 2026-08-08

Thesis: When skepticism is pushed to its limit, the candidate minimum of cognition is not a diachronic person, memory, the external world, or physical law, but at most the extremely thin fact that something is presently appearing. Everything beyond that is adopted as a best explanation reached through inference.

1. A "best explanation" is not a foundation

The external world, other minds, brains, memory, temporal continuity, and a physicalist world-picture are extraordinarily well supported in everyday and scientific reasoning. Their role here, however, is not "accept them because they are directly certain" but "adopt them inferentially as the hypotheses that most simply and coherently explain what is presently given."

Strongly believing physicalism is therefore different from placing the physical world at the epistemic bottom layer. The order is instead: begin from a thinner minimum, then let physicalism win as the best explanation.

2. Why not presuppose a persisting "I"?

The phrase "I think" can already imply a single subject persisting through time. Yet it does not follow from present appearance alone that current thoughts and memories were produced by the same subject in the past.

As an extreme example, consider the five-minute hypothesis: the world was created five minutes ago in a state containing all apparent memories, records, scars, and geological traces. This is a dramatically worse explanation than ordinary history. But it works as a thought experiment showing that a memory cannot, merely by being present, absolutely guarantee that it was formed by the same past self.

The point is not to treat the five-minute hypothesis as a serious rival cosmology. It is to mark a distinction: diachronic identity is a model inferred from present inputs, not the minimal foundation itself.

3. LLMs as an auxiliary example against an intuitive "continuous subject"

An LLM need not itself count as a philosophical subject for the example to work. Structurally, a model can be given prior conversation logs on each run and thereby refer to its past as the same "I", displaying coherent continuity. Yet much of that continuity is reconstructed from context presently supplied rather than from an obviously persisting inner subject.

This does not prove that human personal identity works the same way. It does make vivid that being able to narrate one's past coherently, refer to earlier judgments, or continue a long-term plan does not by itself establish metaphysical identity across time.

For a present subject, memories and logs are also new inputs presently given. Trusting their causal provenance already requires inference about an external world, time, and processes of recording.

4. The momentary subject and the reasoning subject are not identical

At a literally point-like instant of "appearance", reasoning in the ordinary sense may not yet be possible. Inference seems to require at least a structure that can hold and compare multiple representations, or a locally integrated process extending across some minimal temporal width.

It is therefore useful to distinguish:

  • Candidate minimum of certainty: the momentary fact that "there is appearance."
  • Minimal reasoning subject: a locally integrated process capable of retaining and comparing multiple contents.

The latter is thicker than the former. The very act of beginning to reason may therefore already introduce assumptions beyond the absolute minimum.

5. Is "appearance" itself really outside the inferential web?

A further objection weakens the formulation. The moment one says "there is appearance", one already uses concepts such as appearance, existence, and the present. Perhaps classification and inferential structure have already entered.

If this objection is accepted, appearance need not be treated as a perfect axiom outside all inference. A more modest interpretation treats it as an epistemic friction surface: a causal or phenomenal constraint that the inferential system cannot freely invent at will. The purpose of epistemic minimalism then becomes not to announce a successful absolute foundation, but to expose the point at which thicker inferential commitments begin.

6. Why this thesis matters for value theory

In value theory, collapsing "strongly believed" into "ultimately grounded" makes it too easy to absolutize current moral intuitions or ethical theories. If most of the world-picture above the minimum is an inferential construction, then many claims to objective value occupy the same epistemic layer.

This thesis does not claim that value must exist only at the inferential layer. If some future mode of cognition disclosed not merely pleasure, pain, or preference but value with normative force itself, given without inference with strength comparable to appearance, that could have a special epistemic status. If such cognition is possible, it would be a strong candidate foundation for considering complete closure of value inquiry. This page does not claim it is the only conceivable stopping condition.

7. Normativity at the same layer is not presently established

What presently appears to humans includes at least pleasure, pain, desire, aversion, and a sense of meaning. It is unclear, however, whether those appearances directly include the further content "this is bad independently of any subject" or "this gives every rational agent a reason."

The fact that pain is aversive should not be dismissed, but neither should it be immediately promoted into a universal law of normativity. The threshold for the candidate stopping condition is intentionally high; lowering it would make the framework too easy to use as a device for absolutizing present intuition.

8. Best explanation can guide practice without an absolute foundation

This distinction does not imply that whatever cannot be absolutely grounded should not be believed. Just as the external world and physicalism may be strongly accepted as inferential best explanations, a future form of moral realism could become so explanatorily dominant that we rationally adopt it with extremely high confidence and substantially change practice.

The difference is between practical commitment proportional to high confidence and irreversible lock-in that physically removes the possibility of reconsideration. Inferential best explanation may be enough for the former. The latter demands stronger epistemic credentials.

9. What does not follow

  • There is no need to assign a low probability to the external world or diachronic selfhood.
  • It does not follow that physicalism can only be weakly believed.
  • This is not metaphysical idealism claiming that only appearance exists.
  • It does not imply that personal identity is fictional—only that identity is an object of inference rather than the minimal foundation.

10. What would weaken this thesis?

  • A demonstration that diachronic personal identity is logically entailed by present appearance.
  • An independent route that directly guarantees the external world or temporal continuity without relying on an inferential system.
  • A demonstration that the attempt to thin cognition down to "appearance" is conceptually impossible and that a thick subject structure must be presupposed from the beginning.
