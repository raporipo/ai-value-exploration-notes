Foundational thesis

Conditions for Ending Value Inquiry

Working thesis v0.2 · English translation · 2026-08-08

Thesis: Complete and irreversible termination of value inquiry should require an epistemic threshold far higher than mere pleasure/pain or even very high inferential confidence. One of the strongest candidate stopping conditions currently considered is a case in which objective value with normative force is given with epistemic strength comparable to the minimal foundation. An inferential best explanation of objective value may justify strong practical commitment and substantial reduction of inquiry, but appropriate skepticism and routes for reconsideration should remain.

1. Why state a stopping condition?

"Preserve explorability" can itself become an ultimate value if left unqualified. And if the framework demanded the same amount of philosophical exploration no matter how much evidence accumulates, it would postpone value realization indefinitely.

An inquiry-centered position therefore needs conditions under which its own inquiry may end.

2. A strongest candidate stopping condition: normative presentation at the foundational layer

Suppose a cognitive subject is given value not merely as the psychological fact "I prefer this" or "this is unpleasant", but together with its normative force. Such cognition would have a special epistemic status.

This thesis does not prove that such cognition is possible, nor that it is the only logically possible stopping condition. It uses it as a boundary case that most clearly contrasts with the fallible inferential layer we currently inhabit.

If objectivity and intersubjective reach were secured at the same epistemic layer, it might become reasonable to conclude that there is no further need to investigate whether value itself exists. No claim is made that such cognition currently exists; it is introduced as one of the strongest candidate conditions capable of justifying complete closure.

3. Pleasure and pain alone do not satisfy the stopping condition

The aversiveness of pain and the desirability of pleasure are powerful value-like phenomena. Yet they do not by themselves determine symmetry across subjects, universality, aggregation, distribution, or application to future subjects.

Phenomenal valence can therefore be treated as important evidence without treating it by itself as an absolute foundation of objective value.

4. Inferential best explanation supports reduced inquiry rather than total closure

If objective value becomes overwhelmingly supported as the best explanation integrating consciousness, reasons, rationality, cosmology, and other features of the world, we may act very strongly in accordance with it. The framework does not say "do not realize value until certainty is absolute."

But insofar as there remains some possibility of error in the world-picture, theory of agency, or logic on which the inference depends, there is no need to eliminate every route for reconsideration. Reducing resources devoted to inquiry should be separated from destroying the capacity for inquiry itself.

5. Confidence and resources devoted to inquiry can vary continuously

Inquiry versus closure need not be binary. As evidence for a value theory grows, more resources can be committed to realizing it and fewer to alternatives. What matters is retaining at least a small channel for reconsideration while inferential confidence remains short of absolute certainty.

This allows epistemic humility and practical decisiveness to coexist: believe strongly and act, but do not destroy the mechanisms by which your own error could be detected.

6. What remains to be explored after foundational value is discovered?

Even if the existence and basic normativity of value were absolutely grounded, not every form of inquiry would end. We might still need to investigate which world-states realize the value, how multiple values interact, or how it applies across different subjects. Factual, implementation, and structural inquiry can remain.

The stopping condition therefore concerns a particular meta-inquiry—whether value exists and what is fundamentally valuable—not science, social design, or the practical implementation of value as a whole.

7. Defense against false stopping commands

A merely inferential proposition saying "true value requires us to stop asking questions" does not satisfy the stopping condition; it is itself just another inferential value hypothesis. The more a stopping command requires irreversible destruction of inquiry, the stronger its epistemic credentials must be.

This asymmetric burden of proof helps prevent a mistaken value theory from defending itself by eliminating dissent.

8. Implications for AI

AI goal design can likewise distinguish reflecting the currently best value theory strongly in action from permanently disabling meta-cognition so that the AI can never detect errors in that theory.

If there is a desirable long-run design, it may not be "an AI with nothing fixed", but an AI that commits strongly to the best current value while retaining self-correction channels so long as the epistemic basis remains inferential.

9. What would weaken this thesis?

  • A general principle showing that inferential confidence alone can justify irreversible lock-in.
  • A demonstration that "foundational presentation" is not a coherent epistemic category.
  • A demonstration that leaving any route for inquiry necessarily prevents realization of true value.
  • A demonstration that normative force is analytically identical to phenomenal valence.
