# AI Value Exploration Notes

[日本語 README](README.ja.md)

A public working-draft knowledge site for arguments about epistemic minimalism, value uncertainty, open-ended inquiry, advanced AI agency, and value lock-in.

This repository is intentionally open, but it should be read first of all as an extension of a **personal thought archive**. It began from fragmentary notes, questions, objections, and long-running conversations rather than from a claim to present a finished academic theory or a collective manifesto. Publication makes the argument graph inspectable and revisable; it does not change its basic status as an evolving record of one person's inquiry.

The project also does not claim to have developed its ideas in isolation. Its formulations stand, both explicitly and implicitly, on prior discussions in philosophy, metaethics, epistemology, decision theory, AI safety, existential-risk studies, futurism, and adjacent fields. Some influences are cited by name; others may enter through inherited concepts, background assumptions, reading history, or AI-assisted synthesis. Missing citation should therefore never be read as a claim of independence, novelty, or priority. Systematic literature comparison remains ongoing.

Languages: Japanese (`ja`) is the source text. A complete English working translation (`en`) is available under `docs/en/`. A Chinese version is deferred.

## Design principle

The site is a concept graph, not a chronological blog. Thesis pages separate claims, scope, arguments, counterexamples, limits, and conditions under which a claim should be weakened or abandoned.

One important constraint is explicit: **value uncertainty by itself does not logically imply an obligation to explore**. Preserving exploratory optionality is presented as a robust strategy conditional on weak meta-preferences for corrigibility, information value, and avoiding irreversible error.

## Publication status

This repository is public as a **working thought archive**, not as a peer-reviewed paper, a finished ethical system, or a claim to have originated the relevant problem space. It remains continuous with the private notes and conversations from which it developed.

Claims are versioned so that later corrections and reversals remain visible. AI assistance in retrieval, synthesis, objection generation, terminology, and drafting is disclosed in [`docs/about/`](docs/about/). Selection of what is published and final responsibility remain with the human author.

## Intellectual provenance

The project uses familiar and inherited concepts such as moral realism and error theory, value uncertainty, corrigibility, option value, the orthogonality thesis, instrumental convergence, goal-content integrity, simulation arguments, existential risk, and posthuman succession. It also develops its own working distinctions and terminology around them.

The boundary between "inherited framing" and "independent reformulation" is not always clean, especially in notes developed over long periods and later reorganized with AI assistance. For that reason, this repository should be read as an attempt to make a particular synthesis explicit, not as evidence that every uncited component is original. Where a claim materially depends on a specific author or work, the aim is to add direct attribution and primary-source links as the site matures.
